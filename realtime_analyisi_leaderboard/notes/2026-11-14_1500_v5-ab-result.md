# 14 Nov, 15:00 CET — A/B result: the learned ranker (v5) beats the rules (v4), on known shoppers

**Result.** In 23 minutes of split traffic (13:34–13:57 UTC), `skore_money_v5` converted **2.50%** of the
requests it served and `skore_money_v4` **1.50%**. The difference is outside the noise, and it comes from
known shoppers — where the whole gap to the reference was.

| Shoppers | v5 (learned ranker) | v4 (rules) | Difference | In standard errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Known | 2.10% (35 purchases / 1,665 requests) | 0.97% (13 / 1,340) | +1.13 points | **2.6** |
| New | 3.50% (23 / 657) | 2.68% (16 / 596) | +0.82 points | 0.8 (not conclusive) |
| All | 2.50% (58 / 2,322) | 1.50% (29 / 1,936) | +1.00 points | **2.4** |

Realised purchases, from `team_06.feedback`; `tick.py --since "2026-11-14 13:35:00"`, read at 13:59 UTC.
The model session moved all traffic to v5 at 13:57 UTC, with a small exploration arm (`skore_money_v5_explore`).

## Why it works — the mechanism from the 14:30 note, confirmed

Known shoppers, products they had not bought:

| | v5 | v4 |
|---|---|---|
| Share of impressions in a subcategory where the shopper has bought before | **62%** (10,370 of 16,660) | 34% (4,561 of 13,400) |
| Purchases per impression inside those subcategories | 0.222% (23) | 0.219% (10) |
| Purchases per impression outside them | **0.191%** (12) | 0.034% (3) |

Two effects, both in the direction the analysis predicted: v5 shows almost twice as many products from the
shopper's purchase subcategories, and when it goes outside them it chooses far better than the rules did.

## Against the reference

| | Overall | Cold / new shoppers | Known shoppers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reference (leaderboard 15:01 CET) | 2.06 | 1.85 | ≈ 2.16 (implied) |
| v5 on the requests it served | 2.50 ± 0.64 | 3.50 ± 1.4 | 2.10 ± 0.69 |
| v4 on the requests it served | 1.50 | 2.68 | 0.97 |

(± is two standard errors.) v5 is at the reference's level on known shoppers and at or above it overall. The
leaderboard still shows 1.50 because its window is one hour and most of that hour was v4; it should climb
during the next hour if reliability holds. The leaderboard counts expected purchases, ours are realised ones:
they agree on average, not minute by minute.

## Does it generalise to the final?

- The gain is on known shoppers. With new shoppers v5 is not worse (3.50% vs 2.68%), but 23 against 16
  purchases does not prove it is better. That is the number to watch, because the final brings new shoppers.
- v5's shopper features are counted from the live stream as well as from the 60 days, which is what makes the
  lever available to a shopper first seen during the final (it gave 5.8× with live-only history, 14:25 note).
- Still untested: a shopper population with no 60-day history at all. A check worth running offline before
  Sunday: score the held-out live feedback with the shoppers' 60-day history blanked out, and compare v5 with v4.
