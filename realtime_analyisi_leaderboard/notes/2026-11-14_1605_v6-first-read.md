# 14 Nov, 16:05 CET — v6 is live: first read, and the brand lever is not in its lists yet

`skore_money_v6` (shopper × brand history, new-product and discount features) went live at 14:55:12 UTC as an
A/B against `skore_money_v5`. Six minutes of traffic — a direction, not a result.

## Same six minutes, every arm

| Arm | Known shoppers | New shoppers |
|---|---|---|
| v5 | 1.67% (6 purchases / 360 requests) | 2.31% (4 / 173) |
| v6 | **0.26% (1 / 379)** | **4.61% (7 / 152)** |
| v6 exploration | 0 / 58 | 2 / 35 |

On known shoppers v6 is behind (6 purchases against 1; about a 6% chance if the two were equal); on new
shoppers it is ahead (7 against 4, not conclusive). About 40 more minutes are needed.

## The mechanism check: what each arm shows known shoppers

| Share of impressions that are… | v5 | v6 |
|---|---|---|
| of a brand the shopper has bought | 49.2% | **48.0%** |
| in a subcategory the shopper has bought | 62.0% | **57.2%** |
| products added during the event | 3.6% | 5.8% |
| already bought by the shopper | 0% | 0% |
| The two most frequent slot-1 products | 6% and 5% of requests | 10% and 9% |

v6 has the brand features, but its lists do not contain more products of bought brands than v5's, and they
contain fewer from bought subcategories. What moved is the share of new products and the weight of the global
best sellers on slot 1. So today's A/B is not a test of the brand lever: the lever is not being pulled.

Why that can happen: the model learns from what was shown. Bought-brand products were shown to shoppers who
were going to see them anyway (they sit inside their subcategories), so the training rows may not separate
brand from subcategory; and the retrieval lift (×2) decides only which 500 candidates enter, not their order.
The direct evidence for the lever is the per-impression table below, which the ranker has to be made to follow.

## The brand effect holds when the shopper's purchase count is held fixed

Known shoppers, impressions served by v4 and v5 since 12:40 UTC (exploration excluded), products not bought.
Purchases per impression:

| Purchases in the 60 days | Neither | Subcategory only | Brand only | Both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | 0.055% (21 / 38,172) | 0.067% (8 / 11,864) | **0.149%** (8 / 5,377) | **0.166%** (10 / 6,037) |
| 10–19 | 0.087% (10 / 11,550) | 0.124% (8 / 6,474) | **0.189%** (8 / 4,228) | 0.160% (15 / 9,398) |
| 20–39 | 0.095% (6 / 6,341) | 0.069% (3 / 4,371) | 0.122% (4 / 3,275) | **0.234%** (30 / 12,803) |
| 40+ | 0.272% (9 / 3,308) | 0.165% (4 / 2,422) | 0.129% (3 / 2,317) | 0.248% (43 / 17,353) |

- For shoppers with **1–19 purchases** — the ones v5 did not improve — the brand is the signal: a bought brand
  sells 2–2.7× more, with or without the subcategory, while the subcategory alone adds almost nothing.
- For heavy buyers the pair "subcategory and brand" is what sells; with 40+ purchases nearly everything does.
- So the effect is not a by-product of "this shopper buys a lot".

## What to do

1. Let the A/B run to about 16:45 CET before judging v6 overall; read known and new shoppers separately.
2. Whatever the A/B says, make the lever visible in the lists: for shoppers with fewer than 20 purchases,
   rank bought-brand products up directly (a multiplier on the final score, as the rules did for carts), then
   check that the share of bought-brand impressions rises above 49% and whether their conversion follows.
3. v6's gain on new shoppers, if it holds, is worth keeping on its own: an arm that uses v6 for new shoppers
   and v5 for known ones is a legitimate option.

For the final: the brand of a purchase is known the moment it happens in the live stream, so the lever exists
for shoppers first seen in the final; new shoppers are the larger risk there, and v6 looks better on them.
